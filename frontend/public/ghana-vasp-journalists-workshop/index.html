Why this matters

The rules are taking shape now.

Ghana is replacing years of regulatory uncertainty with formal oversight of its digital-asset economy. As the framework for Virtual Asset Service Providers moves into implementation, most of the public will learn what the rules mean through the press.

But digital-asset coverage often collapses into cryptocurrency prices, fraud and enforcement. The harder questions—licensing, AML/CFT, consumer protection, competition, entrepreneurship and financial inclusion—receive far less attention.

Will Ghanaian startups and entrepreneurs have a workable route into the regulated market—or will compliance and capital requirements leave the sector to large, well-capitalised firms?

AWI position
Ghana needs rules strong enough to protect consumers and the financial system—and open enough for responsible Ghanaian entrepreneurs to build inside the regulated economy.

AWI's focus is credible, proportionate and evidence-based regulation. Licensing terms are being shaped now. Journalists who understand the framework can ensure that the trade-offs around competition, innovation, consumer protection and market access receive serious public scrutiny.

FAQ

Before you apply.

Who is the workshop for?

Working journalists and editors covering business, finance, technology, public policy, investigations, fintech, banking, payments or entrepreneurship.

Do I need prior blockchain or crypto knowledge?

No. The programme is designed to build regulatory literacy from the ground up. A strong interest in financial policy, technology, regulation or economic reporting is sufficient.

Is this a cryptocurrency trading workshop?

No. It is a specialist policy and regulatory journalism programme focused on Ghana's VASP framework, implementation, public-interest reporting and proportionate regulation.

What will participants receive?

Selected participants receive access to the full two-day programme, specialist briefings, a practical media toolkit, relevant expert access, networking and a certificate of participation.

Where will the workshop take place?

The workshop will be held in Accra, Ghana, on 2–3 December 2026. The final venue will be communicated to selected participants.
